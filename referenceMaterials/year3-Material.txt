Detailed breakdown of the english topics:
### 1. Reading: Word Reading
*   **Applying Phonics:** Using their knowledge of phonics to quickly decode unfamiliar words.
*   **Exception Words:** Reading and spelling common exception words (words that do not follow standard phonetic rules) appropriate for Year 3/4.
*   **Fluency and Expression:** Reading aloud with increasing fluency, accuracy, and expression, paying attention to punctuation (e.g., pausing at commas, changing tone for question marks).
*   **Reading for Pleasure:** Encouraging independent reading of a wide range of fiction, poetry, plays, non-fiction, and reference books.

### 2. Reading: Comprehension
*   **Retrieval:** Finding and copying information directly from the text.
*   **Inference:** Drawing inferences about characters’ feelings, thoughts, and motives from their actions, and justifying these inferences with evidence from the text.
*   **Prediction:** Predicting what might happen based on details stated or implied in the text.
*   **Summarising:** Identifying main ideas drawn from more than one paragraph and summarising them.
*   **Vocabulary in Context:** Identifying how language, structure, and presentation contribute to meaning (e.g., understanding why an author chose a specific word).
*   **Using Dictionaries:** Beginning to use dictionaries to check the meaning of unfamiliar words.

### 3. Writing: Composition
*   **Planning:** Discussing and recording ideas before writing.
*   **Drafting:** Writing structured narratives (stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end) and non-chronological reports or persuasive texts.
*   **Evaluating and Editing:** Proofreading their own and others’ writing for spelling and punctuation errors, and suggesting improvements to vocabulary or grammar.
*   **Reading Aloud:** Reading their own writing aloud to a group or the whole class, using appropriate intonation and controlling the tone and volume so that the meaning is clear.

### 4. Writing: Transcription (Spelling & Handwriting)
*   **Spelling Rules:** 
    *   Adding suffixes beginning with *-ing*, *-ed*, *-er*, *-est* (e.g., *hop* → *hopping*, *big* → *bigger*).
    *   Using the prefix *un-* (e.g., *happy* → *unhappy*).
    *   Using the suffixes *-ment*, *-ness*, *-ful*, *-less*, *-ly*.
    *   Spelling words with the /ʃ/ sound spelt with *ch* (e.g., *chef*, *machine*) or *ti* (e.g., *nation*).
*   **Homophones:** Understanding and correctly spelling near-homophones and common homophones (e.g., *there/their/they’re*, *to/too/two*).
*   **Handwriting:** Developing a fluent, legible, and eventually joined (cursive) handwriting style. Learning to use diagonal and horizontal strokes to join letters, and ensuring consistency in the size, shape, and orientation of letters.

### 5. Vocabulary, Grammar, and Punctuation (VGP)
This is a major focus in Year 3. Students learn to consciously apply these rules to improve their writing.
*   **Word Classes:** Identifying and using nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs.
*   **Expanded Noun Phrases:** Adding adjectives and prepositional phrases to make descriptions more detailed (e.g., changing "the butterfly" to "the delicate, blue butterfly on the leaf").
*   **Conjunctions:** Using conjunctions to link clauses, specifically: *when, if, that,* and *because*.
*   **Prepositions:** Using words that show time and place (e.g., *before, after, during, in, because of*).
*   **Fronted Adverbials:** Starting a sentence with a word or phrase that tells the reader *when*, *where*, or *how* something happened, followed by a comma (e.g., "*Later that day,* I went to the park." or "*Without warning,* the door opened.").
*   **Determiners:** Using words like *a, an, the, this, that, these, those* to introduce a noun.
*   **Punctuation:**
    *   Using capital letters, full stops, question marks, and exclamation marks correctly.
    *   Using **commas** to separate items in a list (e.g., *I bought apples, bananas, and grapes.*).
    *   Using **apostrophes** for singular possession (e.g., *the girl’s name*) and for standard contractions (e.g., *isn’t, don’t, I’ll*).

### 6. Spoken Language
*   Listening and responding appropriately to adults and peers.
*   Asking relevant questions to extend understanding and knowledge.
*   Articulating and justifying answers, arguments, and opinions.
*   Participating in discussions, presentations, performances, and role-play.
